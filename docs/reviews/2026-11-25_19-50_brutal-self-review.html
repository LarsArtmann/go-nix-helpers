BRUTAL SELF-REVIEW · 2026-11-25

go-nix-helpers × BuildFlow: two tools, no contract

What is wrong with go-nix-helpers as BuildFlow's sibling, what it costs the fleet today, and the smallest set of changes that makes the two tools agree. Every claim was checked against source, CI logs or a live run on 2026-11-25. Anything not checked is labelled UNVERIFIED.

00Summary

Master CI is red at HEAD. A BuildFlow run re-indented all seven shell scripts to tabs; go-standard's treefmt is pinned to two spaces; the Format Check job fails. That is the visible symptom and it is one command away from fixed.

The cause is structural. go-nix-helpers and BuildFlow overlap in five areas (formatting, vendorHash repair, toolchain environment, templ codegen, flake linting) and share no machine-readable contract. Each side encodes its assumptions about the other in comments. The 61 consumers encode the fallout in workarounds and in .buildflow.yml skips.

go-nix-helpers itself cannot pass BuildFlow today: a dry run fails 9 of 139 steps with 44 findings, nearly all noise from test fixtures. The reference consumer is not a BuildFlow citizen.

The bottom line Three decisions unlock most of the value: pick one shell canon, pick one vendorHash repair owner, and publish from go-standard the four facts BuildFlow currently guesses (formatter ownership, toolchain environment, hash locations, the pinned templ CLI).

◆What already works

+
Upstreaming happens and consumers delete code The stray-symlink sanitizer moved into mkPreparedSource and BuildFlow deleted its wrapper (BuildFlow/flake.nix:387-389). Several consumers record "fixed upstream in go-nix-helpers" for templ and sub-module fixes.
+
The hermetic gates are real checks.vendor-hash really depends on the vendor FOD: evaluated in go-output, the go-modules derivation is an input of the check. A stale hash fails there first.
+
One module carries 61 consumers A self-hosting flake, 72 pure-function checks, 228 to 237 module assertions, a real-backend container smoke test.
+
Single pins exist where it matters templCliPackage is one pin for build, devShells and treefmt; goPkgAttr resolves the newest Go instead of a hardcoded branch.

01Decisions for the owner

These three reverse or settle positions already written down in the two repos. They are yours to make; each has my pick and the evidence.

D1 · Blocking

Shell indentation: two spaces or tabs

modules/go-standard.nix:1972-1973 · .editorconfig:8

go-standard pins treefmt's shfmt to two spaces plus simplify (decided 2026-11-18 to stay stable across treefmt-nix relocks). Everything else in the fleet produces tabs: shfmt's own default, treefmt-nix's current default, BuildFlow's shfmt lane, and the .editorconfig template whose [*] rule says tab in 200 of 224 repos. 38 of the 43 consumers with classifiable shell scripts are tab-indented; 5 are two-space. Only two consumers enable enableShfmt at all.

Pick: tabs. Pin the module default to tabs explicitly (keeps lock independence, aligns with every other writer). One consumer, standard-bug-tracking-schema, has 24 two-space scripts and can override locally.

Alternative: keep two spaces. Run nix fmt, add a two-space [*.sh] rule to .editorconfig, pin tool_options.shfmt.options in .buildflow.yml, and wait for the BuildFlow ownership fix. Dismissed because it keeps three config sites in sync by hand and still loses to any bare shfmt -w.

D2 · Blocking

One vendorHash repair owner

modules/go-standard.nix:1445-1481 · BuildFlow tools/providers/nix_hash_fix_detect.go:58-114

Three implementations exist: BuildFlow's nix-hash-fix, the module's apps.update-vendor-hash, and private apps in 10 consumers. The module's app rewrites exactly one literal vendorHash = "<old>"; line in flake.nix, so it cannot serve the 15 consumers that write vendorHash = import ./vendorHash.nix;, nor the per-system vendorHashes option the module itself offers. The two repos give opposite advice: the BuildFlow skill says "never hand-fix, that is nix-hash-fix's job"; go-nix-helpers TODO_LIST.md:57 (T76) tells bank-sync to adopt the module app.

Pick: nix-hash-fix owns the repair. It is the superset (literal attributes, bare-hash files, several packages, journal and rollback) and it already has tests. go-standard stops shipping an algorithm and publishes hash locations instead (Step 4 and Step 5). BuildFlow must learn the per-system attrset shape.

Alternative: the Nix app owns it. Dismissed because it would need the multi-package and hash-file logic BuildFlow already has, and BuildFlow's pre-commit and verify loop would still call its own.

D3 · Important

One distribution policy

CHANGELOG.md:81-85 · AGENTS.md:27,51 · templates/go-standard/flake.nix:18

Four policies are in writing: the CHANGELOG says pin the tag; AGENTS.md, README and the template say git+ssh://…?ref=master; the owner directive of 2026-10-05 (cited in BuildFlow/flake.nix:12-18) says all LarsArtmann inputs track latest; go-aichat says "never track master here". Per the fleet audit (not recounted), 59 of 69 explicit inputs track a branch, 9 are SHA-pinned and 1 is tag-pinned; 60 of the 61 go-standard consumers use the github: scheme the docs never show. Those 61 lock six different revisions (48 at e0c4b70); SystemNix carries 20 lock nodes at 3 revisions (audit).

Pick: latest-tracking plus tagged releases. Keep the 2026-10-05 directive, cut v0.2.0 now (26 untagged commits, including the post-tag fix e0c4b70 that the CHANGELOG does not record), and make every doc show the same URL.

02Findings

Ordered by impact. Each ends with the plan step that fixes it.

Blocking · High impact

F1. Two formatters own the same shell scripts and BuildFlow cannot see the other one

BuildFlow/tools/providers/format_ownership.go:97-141 · modules/go-standard.nix:1953-1975, 444-452

Symptom (verified). CI run 45669527657, job check (ubuntu-latest), step Format Check: "formatted 16 files (7 changed)". Commit 2e8d1c7 re-indented all seven scripts/*.sh to tabs (whitespace-only for all but check-rows.py). Master was green at e0c4b70.

Mechanism (verified in source). BuildFlow defers a formatter lane to treefmt only when inspectTreefmtOwnership finds treefmt.toml, .treefmt.toml, a root treefmt.nix, or a literal treefmt = { block in flake.nix. go-standard declares treefmt inside its module file. In go-nix-helpers BuildFlow prints treefmt: no files matching and runs its shfmt lane un-deferred (shfmt -w, no indent flag, additional_tools.go:22-32). shfmt defaults to tabs and also reads .editorconfig, whose [*] rule says indent_style = tab. treefmt's shfmt is pinned to two spaces. Two writers, one file type, opposite settings.

Blast radius. The same blindness affects all 61 consumers for the lanes shfmt, prettier-format, oxfmt, dprint-format, templ-fmt and d2-fmt. For shell it is latent in 59 of them because enableShfmt defaults to false.

The docs blame the wrong actor. AGENTS.md:121 and :128 attribute the drift to "a concurrent session (its editor's shfmt config differs)". The 18:52 fingerprints (tab shfmt, dprint emphasis rewrites in 25 docs, a ruff line wrap) match a buildflow --fix --build-mode=full run, and a sweep with exactly that command line was running from an SSH session during this review (actor inferred, not observed).

BuildFlow reports green while CI is red. buildflow -s nix-fmt passes here ("formatted 18 files (0 changed)") because its round-trip copy carries only *.nix files.

Fix: D1, then Step 3a and Step 4 Settle the canon now. Upstream, BuildFlow must treat "flake imports a module, no static treefmt config" as unknown and defer conservatively, which is already its stated doctrine (format_ownership.go:17-23), and resolve exactly through the exported config path.
Blocking · High impact

F2. vendorHash repair has three implementations and a record of damage

See D2 · consumer AGENTS.md files in go-aichat, CV, pbx-lens

Important · Medium impact

F3. go-nix-helpers is linted as a Go application and cannot go green

BuildFlow/language/file_detection.go:16-25 · constants/constants.go:169 · test-assets/

buildflow --dry-run --verbose here: "BuildFlow failed go-nix-helpers 105/139 (44 findings, 3 skipped via config, 51 not applicable)". Nine steps fail: cqrs-lint, erraudit and samber-linter, each on the root and on three modules. Every failure is on test-assets/mock-templ-*, fixtures that are deliberately stale or missing generated code. Go is detected from any .go file in the tree; testdata is skipped, test-assets is not.

nix-checker reports 32 findings, all false on this repo: 7 errors (one empty-hash-attr on the vendorHash option definition at go-standard.nix:493, six fake-hash on test and template placeholders) and 25 info or warning suggestions to extract hashes into vendorHash.nix inside the module's own test suite. At error severity they fail the findings gate.

Also noise: go-structure-linter ("go.mod missing (critical)", ".golangci.yml missing (error)"); nix build . on a flake with no default package (packages.x86_64-linux is empty); the Python lanes firing on the single vendored scripts/check-rows.py ("collected 0 items"), which ruff-format already rewrote at HEAD.

Legitimate findings hidden in the noise: no SECURITY.md (error); actionlint 6 (SC2086 at ci.yml:76 and :129, if: false at :273); cue-vet: ci.yml violates the fleet CI policy on matrix runs-on; a dependabot entry missing for test-assets/mock-templ-stale; dprint.json fetches four plugins over the network.

Fix: Step 2, with Step 3b, 3c and 3d Add .buildflow.yml that tells BuildFlow this is a Nix library with fixture directories, fix the real findings, and let BuildFlow own the gates CI currently duplicates.
Important · Medium impact

F4. The toolchain environment is declared in the flake and re-declared by hand

BuildFlow/internal/cli/precommit_types.go:97-102 · BuildFlow/.buildflow.yml:37-42

20 go-standard consumers set goExperiment in flake.nix. Three mirror it in .buildflow.yml (branching-flow, BuildFlow, touchbase); 17 do not. Six repos set GOTOOLCHAIN by hand in .buildflow.yml. BuildFlow's pre-commit template hardcodes export GOEXPERIMENT=jsonv2 and export CGO_ENABLED=1 whatever the flake says. BuildFlow's own config documents the failure class ("phantom drift"). On Go 1.27 jsonv2 is inert, so this is latent today and live on any older toolchain or non-default experiment.

Fix: Step 4, then Step 3g go-standard publishes the resolved environment once; BuildFlow loads it instead of hardcoding.
Important · Medium impact

F5. templ codegen and treefmt disagree on ordering and on the generator

crush-daily/.buildflow.yml:22-28 · BuildFlow TODO_LIST.md:343 (CG3)

At least five consumers skip BuildFlow's templ-generate because it runs after formatting with a PATH templ and re-canonicalises *_templ.go, so checks.format fails afterwards: crush-daily, dnsblockd, go-cqrs-lite, browser-history, nsfw-classifier (crush-daily checked at source, the rest per the fleet audit). 03The seam: who owns what

The principle that removes the split brains: go-nix-helpers is the single source of truth for the Nix and toolchain facts of a Go repo; BuildFlow is the single owner of the quality loop. BuildFlow reads the facts through a small explicit contract instead of guessing or re-implementing them.

go-nix-helpers
facts → contract
4 exports → BuildFlow
loop
Concern Owner today Owner after
Build, vendor FOD, devShell, image, prepared source go-standard go-standard, unchanged
Which formatter owns which file type treefmt config in the module, invisible; BuildFlow lanes guess go-standard publishes; BuildFlow reads and defers
Formatting runs, detect, repair, verify treefmt, BuildFlow lanes, ci.yml, ci-parity.sh BuildFlow loop; nix fmt stays the formatter for treefmt-owned types
vendorHash repair Three implementations nix-hash-fix (D2)
Toolchain environment flake, .buildflow.yml, hook template go-standard publishes; BuildFlow loads
templ codegen and its oracle BuildFlow templ-generate and go-standard freshness check Codegen, then format; one generator pin
Flake lint rules nix-lint.sh and nix-checker One rule set (Step 7)
CI gate for a commit ci.yml and, elsewhere, a BuildFlow job Nix-native legs in ci.yml; everything else in the BuildFlow job

Scoping rule against the scope-creep trap: stop adding options to either tool that absorb the other's job. go-standard's lint, test, vulncheck and freshness checks stay opt-in for hermetic parity; BuildFlow stops growing Nix knowledge that belongs with the module.

04Plan

Ordered by impact divided by effort. Effort is my estimate. Each step states what "done" means.

1

Settle the shell canon and restore CI (D1)

go-nix-helpers, about 30 minutes. With tabs: change the two mkDefault pins in modules/go-standard.nix:1972-1973 to an explicit tab pin, update the option text and the AGENTS.md canon paragraph. Done when nix fmt is a no-op at HEAD and the check (ubuntu-latest) job is green. With two spaces instead: run nix fmt now, fix .editorconfig, add the shfmt options to .buildflow.yml.

2

Make go-nix-helpers a BuildFlow citizen

1 to 2 hours. Add .buildflow.yml that tells BuildFlow this is a Nix library with fixture directories (go_mod_ignore_dirs: [test-assets] or language: nix; confirm the effect with buildflow --dry-run). Add SECURITY.md. Fix the actionlint, cue-vet and dependabot findings. Run buildflow precommit install. Retire scripts/ci-parity.sh and the CI shellcheck job once a BuildFlow job runs in CI; keep nix fmt -- --fail-on-change as the product gate and move the three unique ci-parity legs (templ fixture guard, old-nixpkgs moduleTest, lock freshness) into check_scripts. Done when buildflow exits 0 here. Blocked on the seven nix-checker false positives until Step 3c lands or an exclude covers them.

3

File the BuildFlow fixes

Each diagnosis below is already verified at source level here. (a) Formatter ownership is blind to module-declared treefmt. (b) Go detection and module walks do not skip fixture directories; findGoModDirs and findAllModuleDirs ignore go_mod_ignore_dirs. (c) nix-checker flags option definitions and test placeholders. (d) nix build . on flakes without a default package. (e) nix-hash-fix does not recognise a per-system vendorHashes attrset (source reading, not exercised). (f) CG3: codegen before formatters. (g) The hook template hardcodes GOEXPERIMENT and CGO_ENABLED.

4

Publish the contract from go-standard (proposed API)

About half a day, both repos. Four eval-able outputs, names proposed: flake.treefmtConfigFile (the store path of the generated treefmt.toml, which already exists as config.treefmt.build.configFile), flake.toolchainEnv (GOEXPERIMENT, CGO_ENABLED, GOTOOLCHAIN, GOWORK, GOPRIVATE), flake.vendorHashLocations, and the pinned templ CLI path. BuildFlow reads them with nix eval and falls back to today's behavior with a warning when a flake does not provide them. Done when a BuildFlow dry run in a go-standard consumer prints "deferred: treefmt owns" for the shell and templ lanes.

5

Retire the vendorHash algorithm from go-standard (D2)

1 to 2 hours. Default enableUpdateVendorHashApp to false or turn the app into a pointer that prints the BuildFlow command. Update TODO_LIST.md:57 (T76) and the migration guide.

6

Close the go-standard gaps BuildFlow fills by hand

One pull request per row of the F6 table, about half a day each. Each PR deletes the matching block of BuildFlow's flake.nix, which is its integration test. Fix the apps.fmt condition in the first one.

7

One flake-lint rule set

1 to 2 sessions. Land BuildFlow's M8 (TODO_LIST.md:638): move the nix-checker detection core into a Go module inside go-nix-helpers, fold the live nix-lint.sh rules into it, and delete nix-lint.sh. This needs a decision that go-nix-helpers may host Go code; its AGENTS.md says it contains none. The payoff is structural: the rule set that checks consumer flakes would live next to the option definitions it checks, which removes the 32 false positives here by construction.

8

Distribution (D3)

About 1 hour. Tag v0.2.0. Add Fixed entries for the post-tag commits. Make AGENTS.md, README, the migration guide and the template show one URL form. Ship a .buildflow.yml and SHA-pinned actions in the template. Drop go 1.26 and the manual hash dance from generate-flake.sh.

9

Cleanup

About 1 hour. The T86 row, docs/ci-workflow.yml, the FEATURES script table, the AGENTS.md counts, the stale ROADMAP row, .editorconfig, dprint.json, the dead CI job, and the ghost script. Then tell consumers their workarounds are stale where BuildFlow already fixed the cause: four projectRootFile overrides (BuildFlow copies root markers since 2026-10-25) and erraudit's note that the module's vendor-hash check "never realizes the FOD".

05The 12 questions

Question Answer
1. What did you forget? That BuildFlow is a consumer with 61 siblings. No BuildFlow config, no hook, no cross-tool test, and the docs blamed an editor for BuildFlow's lane.
2. What is stupid that we do anyway? Keep three vendorHash repairers and three gate sets, and hand-sync GOEXPERIMENT between flake.nix and .buildflow.yml.
3. What could you have done better? The 2026-11-18 shfmt pin was decided against treefmt-nix alone. BuildFlow's lane, the editorconfig template and the fleet's actual scripts were not checked.
4. What could you still improve? Steps 2 to 9 of the plan.
5. Did you lie to me? The docs did, three times: "7+ downstream consumers" (61), "a concurrent session's editor" (BuildFlow's shfmt lane), and erraudit's "never realizes the FOD" (it does). This review's own interim count, "59 of 64 consumers are tab-indented", was wrong and is corrected to 38 of 43.
6. How can we be less stupid? One owner per concern, published as data, and tested across tools: a check that nix fmt is a no-op after a BuildFlow fix run.
7. Ghost systems? Six, listed in F9. The concurrent-actors script has value as a BuildFlow check_scripts entry; the fmt app bug is a one-line fix.
8. Scope creep trap? Yes, in both directions: go-standard grew quality gates BuildFlow also owns, and BuildFlow grew Nix knowledge that belongs with the module. See the scoping rule under "The seam".
9. Did we remove something useful? Nothing structural. The 18:53 commit rewrote emphasis marks in 25 docs and a vendored Python file: harmless, but noise that no gate was designed to review. Corrected 2026-11-25 21:10: this is wrong, see Amendments.
10. Split brains? Twelve: shell canon; vendorHash repair; toolchain environment; templ codegen and oracle; dprint versus treefmt file ownership; pin policy; nix-checker versus nix-lint.sh; three gate sets; module versus hand-written checks.vendor-hash; BuildFlow's green versus CI's red on this repo; docs versus reality; T76 versus the BuildFlow skill.
11. Tests? Strong on the Nix side. Zero on the consumer contract: no test runs the module's outputs through BuildFlow, the update-vendor-hash app has no test for the hash-file shape used by 15 consumers, and the assertion count disagrees inside AGENTS.md.
12. Were inherited claims re-verified? Six were checked at their source. False: erraudit's FOD claim; "projectRootFile is hardcoded" (conditional since commit 4518373 per the audit, present at e0c4b70 and HEAD); the editor attribution. Incomplete: "nix fmt formats .nix files". Shipped: symlink sanitization, and the ROADMAP's eval-time floor check (ROADMAP.md:102-104 lists it as an idea; go-standard.nix:1095 implements it).
